Space has quietly become one of the most active build-out zones in defence technology this year, and most of it isn't happening in headlines about astronauts — it's procurement notices, satellite constellations and budget lines.

## NATO's new constellation

Breaking Defense reported in July 2026 that **eight NATO member states have agreed to build a new satellite mega-constellation**, a collective move to reduce reliance on any single ally's space infrastructure for surveillance and communications — part of a broader pattern of NATO members treating resilient, distributed satellite networks as core military infrastructure rather than a nice-to-have.

## The US build-out

On the US side, fiscal 2026 space funding is approaching **$40 billion**, spread across missile-tracking constellations, missile-defence interceptor development under the Golden Dome initiative, and a notable shift toward commercial integration: the **Commercial Augmentation Space Reserve** is transitioning to full-scale operations in 2026, with a target of 20 contracts by year-end to guarantee wartime access to commercial satellite communications capacity. The Space Development Agency is also running four on-orbit servicing demonstrations in 2026 — refuelling, repair, inspection and manoeuvring — technology that matters as much for extending the life of expensive military satellites as for any offensive application.

That last point connects to a harder-edged story: separate reporting describes US Army efforts to prepare for directly countering adversary surveillance satellites, and the US Space Force has been expanding a satellite-surveillance network based in Australia specifically framed around monitoring Chinese anti-satellite capability — a reminder that "space defence" now spans everything from missile tracking to protecting, and threatening, the satellites themselves.

## Why this matters on the ground

None of this is abstract for terrestrial forces. Missile warning, secure communications, and precision navigation and timing all run through these constellations — which is exactly why every major military now treats "resilience in orbit" (lots of smaller, distributed, quickly replaceable satellites, rather than a few large exquisite ones) as a first-order defence-technology priority rather than a niche specialism.
