The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute's newest figures put a hard number on a trend that's been building for over a decade: the world is spending more on its militaries than at any point since the Cold War's end, and the growth is no longer just an American or Chinese story.

## The headline numbers

World military expenditure reached **$2.887 trillion in 2025**, a **2.9% increase** over 2024 and the **eleventh consecutive year of growth** — pushing global military spending to **2.5% of world GDP**, the highest share since 2009. The three largest spenders — the United States, China and Russia — together accounted for **$1.48 trillion, or 51% of the global total**: the US at $954 billion (down 7.5% year-on-year), China at $336 billion (up 7.4%), and Russia at $190 billion (up 5.9%).

## Europe is the real story

The sharpest shift is regional. **European military spending climbed 14% to $864 billion** — described by SIPRI researchers as the steepest annual increase in Central and Western Europe since the end of the Cold War. Individual national figures make the scale concrete: **Germany's defence budget surged 24% to $114 billion**, **Spain increased spending 50% to $40.2 billion**, and **Ukraine's own military expenditure rose 20% to $84.1 billion — a striking 40% of its GDP**. Asia and Oceania weren't far behind, growing **8.1% to $681 billion**, the largest annual increase in that region since 2009, with **Japan's spending up 9.7% to $62.2 billion**. The Middle East, by contrast, stayed close to flat at $218 billion, up just 0.1%.

## What's actually driving it

None of this is happening in a vacuum. The IISS Military Balance 2026, covered separately, frames the same surge against a backdrop of active geopolitical strife and ongoing wars reshaping how governments think about acceptable peacetime defence budgets — Europe's jump in particular tracks closely with the continuing war in Ukraine and a broader recalibration of how much European governments believe they need to spend on deterrence rather than assume US or NATO backstops will cover the gap.

## Why it matters for defence technology specifically

Money at this scale doesn't just buy more of the same equipment — it accelerates the R&D and production-capacity investment behind almost everything else covered on this site, from hypersonic interceptors to submarine-industrial-base expansion to next-generation fighter programmes. When a defence budget grows 24% or 50% in a single year, as Germany's and Spain's did, that's the kind of capital injection that turns a slow-moving research programme into a fielded capability years earlier than it would have arrived otherwise.
