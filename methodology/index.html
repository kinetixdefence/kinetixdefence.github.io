Editorial & fact-checking policy

How a piece of information becomes a published KINETIX article.

No automated system can guarantee a factual claim is never wrong — anyone who tells you otherwise is overselling their tooling. What KINETIX commits to instead is a pipeline designed to catch errors before publication, and a transparent record of what was checked and how, so readers can judge the reporting for themselves.

The pipeline

StageWhat happens
1. Collection Source material is pulled from a curated list of defence-news outlets, official government/ministry channels, and industry releases, across multiple languages.
2. Translation & normalisation Non-English material is translated, with the original-language source retained and linked so it can be independently checked.
3. Corroboration check A factual claim is only drafted into an article if it is independently corroborated by at least two separate sources (or one official/primary source, e.g. a government press release, plus one independent outlet). Single-source claims are flagged as unverified and held back rather than published.
4. Draft & source log A draft article is generated with every factual claim traceable to a source in a visible "Sources cited" box at the bottom of the article — the same box you'll find on every article on this site.
5. Human editorial approval Nothing goes live automatically. Every draft sits in a pending review queue and is only published after a human editor reads it, checks the cited sources, and approves it. This is a hard gate in our publishing infrastructure, not a policy we could forget to follow.
6. Corrections If an error is found after publication, the article is updated with a visible correction note and date. See our corrections log.

What "multi-source verified" means

Articles carrying the ✓ Multi-source verified badge had their central claims corroborated across two or more independent sources at the time of publication, listed in the article's sources box. Analysis, opinion, and forward-looking or speculative reporting (e.g. unconfirmed program capabilities) is explicitly labelled as such in the text rather than presented as confirmed fact.

What we won't publish

  • Classified, leaked, or operationally sensitive information.
  • Technical detail that could meaningfully help someone build, acquire, or use a weapon.
  • Single-source rumours presented as fact.
  • Unlabelled sponsored content.

Report an error

Spotted something wrong? Please tell us — see Corrections for how to flag it, or submit a tip with a source.
